    What Nɛpɛm keeps, and what he does not

    This project exists because AI was careless with a language. Being careless with the people teaching it would be the same mistake.

    Kept

    • Words and corrections sent through the forms above
    • The name and village given with them, if any were given
    • The date it was sent
    • A recording of your voice, if you record a word, and the word it is of
    • A random code your browser makes and sends with each recording, so recordings by the same voice can be grouped. It does not say who you are
    • Whether you said it is a man's or a woman's voice, if you said, and which consent boxes you ticked

    Not kept

    • Conversations with Nɛpɛm. Nɛpɛm does not keep a record of them. To answer, your messages are sent to OpenAI, which may keep them for up to 30 days
    • Any account, email address, or login. There are none
    • Anything at all from the games. They run in the browser and send nothing. The site itself counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, and loads its fonts from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address
    • Anything the microphone hears outside a recording you choose to send. A recording you do not send never leaves your device

    What happens to it

    • A native speaker reads every submission before Nɛpɛm learns it
    • Approved words become part of what Nɛpɛm teaches. That means they are sent to OpenAI with each question. Your name is not sent with them
    • A name and village given with a submission are shown publicly, on this page, in the list of people who have taught Nɛpɛm
    • A speaker listens to every recording before anyone else can play it. Once approved, anyone using this site or the chat can play it
    • A name given with a recording may be shown publicly on the site. The voice you chose is not shown anywhere
    • If you ticked the second box, your recording may also be used to build speech tools for Kenyang
    • Recordings are not sent to OpenAI
    • Submissions, including recordings, are held by Supabase, the database service this site uses
    • To have a submission or a recording removed, write to agboredouard51@gmail.com

    Nɛpɛm is not trained on what people send. There is no learning that happens on its own. A speaker confirms a word, writes it into the material by hand, and only then can Nɛpɛm say it.     Mission and data governance

    Many AI language tools leave African languages out, and some are built from community data that nobody asked for. Nɛpɛm is built the other way round.

    Nɛpɛm starts from published work, above all the lexicon and orthography CABTAL produced, and grows from what speakers give him. Every word and every recording a speaker sends is given voluntarily and checked by a native speaker before Nɛpɛm teaches it or anyone else can hear it.

    Every recording is covered by consent the speaker gives before recording: having it played on this site is one choice, and letting it help build speech tools for Kenyang is a separate one they can decline. Anyone can ask for their recording to be removed.

    Nɛpɛm answers using OpenAI's models, and the verified Kenyang material is sent with each question, so answers are built from that material rather than from what a model guesses. Book words a speaker has not yet checked are sent too, and Nɛpɛm is told to say so when he uses one. Any word in an answer that appears nowhere in the material is flagged under the answer. Recordings are never sent.

    What a speaker confirms goes with every question and outranks everything else Nɛpɛm has read, and when he does not know, he says so.

    It will not invent your language

    Ask a general purpose AI for Kenyang and it answers confidently and wrongly, because it has never seen the language. For anyone learning, that is worse than no answer. The invented words get learned as real ones.
