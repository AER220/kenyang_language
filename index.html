Who made this, and why

I am Agbor Edouard Ransome, from Bachou Ntai in the Manyu Division of Cameroon. Kenyang is my mother tongue.

I study Applied Human-Centered AI at Syracuse University and I build AI for people the technology usually skips. I founded GritGateway, which connects African students to universities and scholarships. I have been a Chevening Scholar and a Mandela Washington Fellow.

I built this because a machine that speaks a hundred languages had never heard mine. I recovered Kenyang from old dictionaries and grammars no computer could read, then checked every word and every rule myself. A tool that invents your language is worse than no tool at all.

What I actually want is a father and a child arguing over a crossword clue in Kenyang, and one of them going to ask the grandmother who is right.

It will not invent your language

Ask a general purpose AI for Kenyang and it answers confidently and wrongly, because it has never seen the language. For anyone learning, that is worse than no answer. The invented words get learned as real ones.

Everything here comes from Kenyang recovered from old sources and then confirmed by a native speaker. When Nɛpɛm does not know something, it says so. That is why there is a whole section above of words it cannot help you with yet.

Kenyang is spoken by about 65,000 people in the Southwest Region of Cameroon. It has never had an app, a course, or a crossword. This is the first.
