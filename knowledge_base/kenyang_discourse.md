# Kenyang — vocabulary, phrases & grammar (from discourse texts)

> Source: Kenyang Hortatory Discourse Collection (SIL, 2004), converted from a
> scan by the project owner. Everything below is drawn from that text's own
> interlinear glosses and free translations — nothing invented.
>
> IMPORTANT for the tutor:
> - The source states tone is written UNSYSTEMATICALLY here. Do NOT present tone
>   marks in this file as authoritative pronunciation; teach the word, and be
>   honest that its exact tone needs a native speaker or the phonology source.
> - Verbs usually appear WITH their subject/tense prefix attached (e.g. "ndɔk" =
>   "I go", not a bare root "go"). Teach them as they appear and explain the
>   prefix using the grammar notes below.
> - Genre is hortatory (community speeches), so vocabulary leans toward
>   family, village, and exhortation. It is thinner on tourist basics.
> - A few entries may carry OCR/alignment slips; verify against the original
>   before publishing anything from here.

## Grammar words
- mɛ — I, me (1S)  ·  wɔ — you (2S)  ·  yi — him, her (3S)
- besɛ / yesɛ — we, us (1P)  ·  beka / bè — you (2P)  ·  bɔ — they, them
- nɛ — with, and
- bɛ / bé — that (introduces a reported clause)
- anɛ / ɛnɛ / ɛbhɛn / abhɛn — who, which, that
- kɛ — but  ·  apú — is not  ·  yɛ — no, not
- mbak / mbɔ / kɛmbak — if
- nkɛm / mankem / akɛm — all, every
- tonto — much, greatly

## Phrases & sentences (real, with translations)
- Bo mma, é è. — Mothers, greetings!  (respectful address to a group of women; "é è" is an accompanying call)
- Mfɔ afu nekɔ atwɔ. — The chief has come from his journey.
- Ayaŋ bérem kɛpi nta bo ɛtɔk. — He wants to talk with the people of the village.
- Mankem ngɔre mbakanɛm nkem mántwɔ́. — Every woman and man should come.
- Bɔ mma nɛ bɔ eta batwɔ. — Mothers and fathers, come.
- Sé nkaka Eta Mándém. — Let us thank Father God.
- Ghɔ ka anɛ ntwɔ nɛwu nɔ. — Listen, all of you — that is what I have come with.
- Ɛchɔŋ, ntwò nɛ ntó… — Today, I come with a message…
- Mě kaka bè tonto. — I greatly thank you.
- Nchyɛ yi babhé. — I give him medicine.
- Ɔ́na nenyɛ ɛnɛn bó manye. — You cook food that people eat.
- Wɔ tesɛ mmik nɛ manyɛp. — You wipe the floor with water.

## Grammar patterns (working analysis — not a full grammar)
- Subject prefix on the verb marks who does it: n-/m- = I, ɔ-/o- = you (sg),
  a- = he/she, sɛ-/se- = we, ba-/be- = you (pl)/they.
  Examples: ndɔk "I go", atwɔ "he comes", sé nkaka "we thank", batwɔ "you come".
- Plural of many nouns uses a be-/ba-/ma- prefix: ngɔre → baghɔre (woman → women);
  ɛtɔk → betɔk (village → villages).
- "Should / let us" (hortatory) uses man-/ma- or se(n)-: mantwó "they should come",
  mandɔk "they should go", sɛnchèm "let us join".
- Tense markers: nyaka = past, chɔŋ = future ("will"), nchí = ongoing ("am/is …ing").
- Linkers: nɛ = "with/and"; bɛ/bé = "that"; anɛ/ɛnɛ/ɛbhɛn = "who/which/that".
- Negation words: kɛ, apú, yɛ.
