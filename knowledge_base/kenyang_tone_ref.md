# Kenyang tone and pronunciation

Source: the phonological section of a published Kenyang study (1998). The
original was written in the International Phonetic Alphabet, which uses symbols
Kenyang spelling does not. It has been converted to Kenyang spelling here, and
the tone is kept separately as a pronunciation note rather than left in the
spelling.

**This is a pronunciation guide, not a spelling guide.** The left column is how
the word is written. The note says how it sounds. Tone marks are not part of
how these words are normally written, so they live in the note, never in the
spelling a learner should copy.

This is a published description converted by hand, not speaker-confirmed for
this project. Where a form here differs from a verified file, the verified form
is the one to use. The recordings, once made, are the real guide to how any of
this sounds; a written description of tone is only a stand-in until then.

---

## Why tone matters: same spelling, different word

These words are spelled the same. Only the tone, high or low, tells them apart.
This is the clearest reason the recordings matter, and the one thing no word
list in the project captures.

    mba         high tone: horn        low tone: wasp
    mbi         high tone: name        low tone: road
    mbok        high tone: deaf        low tone: hole
    ti          high tone: to sell     low tone: to scrape

A learner who says one tone for the other has said a different word. For now
this is written down; when the recordings exist, they will carry it properly.

---

## How a verb's tone changes its tense

The same verb, with the tone alone marking when it happened. Spelling is
unchanged; the tone does the work.

    ti      to sell     he sold (just now): low    he has sold (perfect): high
    nye     to eat      he ate: high                he has eaten: low
    pa      to spit     they are spitting: high     they spat: falling     they have spat: low
    te      to stand    they are standing: low      they stood: falling    they have stood: high
    nisi    to refuse   they are refusing: low-low  they refused: high-low  they have refused: high-high
    so      to wash     they are washing: low       they washed: falling   they have washed: high
    fɛmɛri  to throw    they are throwing: all low  they threw: high-low-low  they have thrown: all high

This is tone carrying grammar, not just meaning. It is why hearing a verb
matters as much as reading it.

## Making one word from another

### A quality or state, from a noun (the kɛ- words)

    nnɔ     mother      →   kɛnɔ        motherhood
    mmirɛ   friend      →   kɛmirɛ      friendship
    nsɛm    slave       →   kɛsɛm       slavery
    mfɔ     chief       →   kɛfɔ        chiefdom
    nnag    in-law      →   kɛnag       in-lawship

### A noun from a verb

    twó     to come     →   nɛtwó       coming
    nyú     to drink    →   nɛnyú       drinking
    té      to burst    →   nɛté        bursting
    sɛp     to descend  →   nɛsɛp       descending
    ti      to sell     →   nɛti        selling
    tó      to send     →   ntó         message
    tɔŋ     to teach    →   ntɔŋ        teacher
    kɔ      to walk     →   nɛkɔ        journey
    nɔkɔ    to respect  →   kɛnɔkɔ      respect
