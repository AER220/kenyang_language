1 Introduction 
 
The Kenyang lexicon has been compiled primarily for speakers of the the Kenyang language in 
order to stimulate interest in the use of the written language, to help new readers and writers and to help 
standardise spelling. It may also be of interest to non-speakers who wish to learn or study the language. 
The Kenyang language is spoken in both Manyu and Meme Divisions in the South West Province. 
The proposed alphabet conforms to the General Alphabet of Cameroon Languages, adopted by the 
national commitee for the unification and harmonisation of the alphabets of Cameroon languages. 
The lexicon contains more than 2,500 entries, all of which come from Lower Kenyang which is the 
reference dialect.   
Each entry consists of the following: the citation form of the Kenyang word, the plural form where 
appropriate, the part of speech, and a translation of the meaning. Verbs are glossed using the infinitive in 
English, but in Kenyang they use the imperative as the citation form. 
A set of appendices is also found including a summary of an overview of the grammar. 
Work on a lexicon is never finished, and so much co-operation is needed from other native speakers 
to expand and improve on this lexicon. 
This lexicon was compiled through the assistance of Mr. Emmanuel M.  Nkongho-Etaka, Mr. 
Andrew Ashu-Eneke and many other villagers. I am very grateful for their assistance. 
 
 
Special thanks goes to Dr Robert Hedinger, SIL consultant, who read this paper and made a lot 
corrections. Without him, this lexicon would not have been completed.

5 Grammatical structures 
 
The focus of the present work is the lexicon of  Kenyang.  However, it is impossible to discuss the lexicon 
without looking at some of the morphosyntactic structures. 
 
5.1 Syntactic structures 
 
Kenyang is a noun class language with a subject, verb and object (SVO) word order. Simple sentence 
structures are shown below: 
 
1) Ató      mmɔ     annywɔ́p 
 hesend  child    at house 
 
 ÜHe sent the child to the houseÜ 
 
 Mmá      a¿ko ∆        njyɛ 
 mother   she climb  hill 
 
 ÜMother is climbing a hillÜ 
 
The noun phrase may consist of a noun as head, followed by modifiers such as adjectives and/or 
determiners. Adjectives in Kenyang are  relatively few in number; most are realised in Kenyang with a 
separate stative clause (e.g. Üthe stone is hardÜ or Üthe stone which is hardÜ, as opposed to the hard 
stoneÜ). Nevertheless, there are true  adjectives for the semantic fields of numbers and colours (see 5.5).  
Noun phrases may also consist of NP-NP constructions in which the first  NP ÜpossessesÜ the second NP. 
In Kenyang just as in many Niger-Congo languages, this construction is marked by an associative marker 
(often only tonal) between the possessor and the possesed. The tone may be H or L. Typical noun phrases 
are as follows: 
 
 N     +    adj   N    +   N 
 bɛtɔk     bɛ́pay   ɛte     ńnok 
 villages  two   pot     soup           
 'two villages'   'a pot of soup' 
 
5.2 Noun 
 
5.2.1 Noun classes 
 
Nouns can be divided into several groups or ÜclassesÜ which are distinguished by their prefixes. These 
classes are numbered according to the system used in all studies of Bantu languages. The class of each 
Kenyang noun with its corresponding plural is shown in the lexicon, for example `ŋv` 'b`s' belongs to 
class 3 and its plural form a`ŋv` belongs to class 6. This is indicated by (n 3/6) after the noun. Some 
nouns belong to the plural class only. In the lexicon, a noun will be found in the lexicon under its singular

5.3 Derived nouns 
 
Derivational processes are limited in Kenyang. The formation of a gerund-type verbal noun are from the 
verb and the formation of an agent are also from the verb. The gerund is formed by adjoining to almost any 
verb stem - transitive or intransitive - the noun class class prefix ná-. Thus, we derive forms such as the 
following: 
 
dɨŋ  'pound'  nɛdɨŋ 'pounding' 
tɔ́ŋ  'teach'  nɛtɔ́ŋ 'teaching' 
rɛm  'say'  nɛrɛm 'saying' 
dók  'jump'  nɛdók 'jumping' 
 
5.4 Pronouns 
 
Kenyang has two types of pronouns which are worthy of note: independent  and possessive pronouns. 
Independent pronouns are those which may stand alone (e.g. in answer to a question such as what? or 
who?). Possessive pronouns are those which make reference to nouns that  ÜpossessÜ other (following)

5.5 Adjectives

marked by a prefix which is governed by the noun class of the noun it qualifies. Adjectives are very few in

5.6 Verb  
 
Kenyang distinguishes four tenses and four aspect markers. Under tenses we can distinguish them by their 
different auxiliaries as will be seen below, while under aspect we can distinguish  them by the change from 
the citation form of the verb that has a high tone. 
In Kenyang, the most free form of the verb is the imperative. The imperative is thus taken as the citation 
form of the verb in this study.
